noisy‑rag‑bench.

Retrieval benchmarks run on clean text. Documents inside a bank or a law firm are scans: OCR confusions, running headers, hyphens across line breaks, redacted spans. Nobody publishes what that does to a RAG pipeline, and it is the normal input for anyone deploying on premise.

Retrieval quality and answer quality come apart under noise, and the gap is invisible to the metric most systems are watched on. Drag the noise below and watch the two separate.

What the chart is showing

recall@5 asks whether the right chunk came back at all. It is the number a retrieval dashboard plots, and under OCR noise it barely moves. Answer recovered asks whether the string that answers the question is still legible inside that chunk. At five percent character error, retrieval is exactly as good as it was on clean text and half the answers are gone.

The two lines are solid and dashed rather than only two colours, so the separation survives being printed, screenshotted, or read by someone who does not see colour.

Figure 2

How much noise matters less than which kind

Character error rate is a poor predictor of damage. Sorted by how badly each condition hurt the answer, not by how noisy it looks.

Page header contamination produces a higher character error rate than the five percent OCR case and costs nothing on either metric, because the added text is uniform and the answer span is untouched. A cleaning budget spent on the noisiest-looking documents would be spent in the wrong place.

Figure 3

Where it loses

Both losing axes, published, because a benchmark that only reports where its author wins is an advertisement.

  • A canonicalized BM25 baseline beats my own hybrid retriever at 14 of 18 conditions, using about a ninth of the memory. The clever retriever is not the thing that helps. results/noise_bench.json
  • The KV-cache half is a negative result. fp16 is free, but int4 costs 11.08% perplexity, flips 16% of greedy decisions, and makes decoding 2.2x slower rather than faster. results/kv_bench.json
  • 132 questions is small. Enough to separate a 0.98 from a 0.51, not enough to rank two retrievers that land a point apart. corpus.py
  • The noise is synthetic. Generated confusion matrices rather than a scanner, chosen so anyone can reproduce it without a corpus they cannot share. noise.py
